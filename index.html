How your dashboard works

One log, one source of truth

Your existing Google Sheet supplies the food and weight data. The dashboard refreshes every 15 minutes. A saved snapshot can keep the page useful when a connection fails; it is always labeled as saved data.

What the numbers mean

Calories are added from meal entries. The daily summary is used when meal calorie values are missing. Disagreements between those two totals are shown for review. “Above range” compares intake with your chosen target, not with calories your body burns.

Protein, carbs and fat are summed from the food log. Blank cells stay unknown. When a note explicitly says an add-on’s macros were counted in another meal, those macros aren’t counted twice. Macro energy uses 4 kcal/g for protein and carbs, and 9 kcal/g for fat; food estimates can differ.

Targets are editable

The original 2,100–2,300 kcal range and 80 kg weight goal are retained. Protein starts at a provisional 128 g. Carbs and fats stay flexible because you haven’t set targets for them. The NIH Body Weight Planner is a resource for exploring personalized calorie plans.

Trends need observations

Averages use logged days with the relevant values present. Empty calendar days are never counted as zero. Having all numeric fields filled doesn’t confirm that every meal was logged. One weigh-in establishes a starting point; this dashboard doesn’t invent a weight-loss forecast.

What stays in your browser

Your preferences and a recent data snapshot. This dashboard doesn’t add analytics trackers or send your log to an AI service. Log and edit actual meals through your Google Sheet.

The rest of the picture

This Sheet does not currently track fiber, sodium, iron, vitamin D, hydration, sleep or activity. No totals or health conclusions are inferred for those.
